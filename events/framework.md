# OSPOlogy framework

Version 2.0 proposal, November 2026.

## Purpose

OSPOlogy brings practitioners and related communities together to exchange experience on open source management, governance, security, sustainability, and AI strategy. Each edition responds to local needs and the capacity of its organizers, in Europe, Asia-Pacific, and other regions.

## Events and meetups

| Category | Organization and setting |
| --- | --- |
| Official OSPOlogy events | Organized or co-organized by TODO Group with local or adjacent communities, at an organization's venue or alongside a Linux Foundation event |
| Community-led OSPOlogy meetups | Led by trusted community organizers; in person, hybrid, or online, around local interests and languages |

“OSPOlogy Live” and “OSPO Local Meetups” are earlier names brought together under the OSPOlogy event brand. Historical materials retain those names. TODO Touchpoints remain separate from this framework.

## Programme

Choose the duration and session mix around the audience, available time, and organizing capacity.

| Duration | Session formats |
| --- | --- |
| One long day or two days | Expert-led presentations or discussion panels, demos, demo workshops, and roundtable discussions |
| Shorter gathering | Keynotes and lightning talks |

For longer programmes, presentations introduce experience and use cases; demos show tools or projects; demo workshops give participants time to try them; roundtables bring peers together around shared questions. Organizers can select the appropriate combination rather than including every format.

### Maturity-based content tiers

Organizers can build the programme around two complementary tracks:

| Track | Focus | Session formats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery Track | Education, awareness, and community building; establish a shared understanding of the OSPO value proposition | A higher proportion of expert-led presentations, keynotes, lightning talks, discussion panels, and introductory demos or guided workshops |
| Implementation Track | Practical collaboration with adjacent communities and projects around a specific industry, technology layer, or shared challenge | Joint demos, demo workshops, featured roundtables, and participant-selected unconference discussions |

For the Implementation Track, bring together OSPO practitioners and relevant project communities to work on concrete use cases, such as open source management in automotive or energy, or across AI infrastructure, cloud native systems, and software supply chains. Use these sessions to connect project expertise with organizational needs and identify practical next steps together.

Combine the tracks according to participants' experience and needs. They describe the balance of content and can be used within one programme, without requiring parallel sessions or a particular event duration.

Adapt the level of explanation and facilitation to participants' experience. Prepare roundtable topics and facilitators in advance when useful. For newcomers, use practical prompts or an [OSPO Book](https://ospobook.todogroup.org/) topic to guide discussion. Topics may also be selected by participants.

The [Amsterdam report](https://todogroup.org/blog/ospologylive-report/) and [Prague recap](https://todogroup.org/blog/2026-10-19-openchain-ospology-prague-2026-recap/) provide examples of presentations, practical sessions, and peer discussion. Use the [reusable toolkit](toolkit/README.md) to prepare the actual programme; session lengths are agreed for each edition.

## Adapting the framework across Linux Foundation communities

Regional Linux Foundation communities and chapters, such as Linux Foundation Japan and Linux Foundation India, can also adapt the OSPOlogy framework to organize joint events across LF projects for a specific region and audience. For example, organizers could bring together AAIF, CNCF, and PyTorch communities, including participants from PyTorchCon, through a shared programme of demos, workshops, and roundtable discussions.

Participating communities can shape the themes, language, and session mix around local needs, with speakers and facilitators from the relevant projects. Agree co-organizing responsibilities and available support with the regional and project contacts, using the same programme guidance and reusable toolkit.

Manage ticketing, registration, and the event programme landing page through Open Community Groups (OCG), within the dedicated instance for the relevant community: LF, LF Europe, LF India, or LF Japan. These pages and ticketing arrangements should be managed by trusted members of Linux Foundation project communities who hold an official ambassador or community meetup organizer role. Coordinate access with the relevant instance administrators.

## Organizing an event

The regional lead coordinates planning with TODO and participating communities. The program committee develops the agenda and recruits speakers and facilitators. The host provides the venue and manages local logistics. Confirm responsibilities, budget, and available TODO/LF support for each edition.

To propose an event, share the location, audience, dates, host, and programme idea with the [organizing team](mailto:ospologylive-organizers@lists.todogroup.org) or through [GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/todogroup/ospology/discussions). Use the toolkit's event brief to record the plan. Agree venue, registration, and admission arrangements with the parent conference for co-located events.

Hosts should actively contribute to open source; an OSPO is preferred. The existing two-thirds organizer vote for workshop requests and organizer participation remains subject to review, with conflicts referred to the TODO Steering Committee. Event frequency and location are proposed according to demand and available capacity.

For standalone events, the host provides the venue at no charge to the organizing team. Participants cover travel, lodging, and meals unless support is confirmed. Agree additional costs and sponsorship with the responsible organizers before committing funds.

### Sponsorship

Standalone OSPOlogy events organized outside Linux Foundation conferences can accept financial or in-kind sponsorship to cover event logistics and attendee materials. Organizations can support AV equipment, recording and media production, lunch, food and beverages, printed materials, swag, and other agreed event costs. Coordinate contributions and acknowledgement with the organizing team using the [sponsorship guide](toolkit/08-Sponsorship/OSPOlogy-Sponsorship-Guide.md).

OSPOlogy events held within or co-located with Linux Foundation conferences, such as KubeCon or PyTorch Conference, follow the parent event's official co-located event or event-track process and sponsorship requirements. Coordinate sponsorship with that event's team before offering opportunities or accepting commitments.

## Community-led meetups

Meetups must be led by trusted community members: TODO ambassadors, ambassadors from related Linux Foundation communities such as AAIG or CNCF, or official community organizers from Linux Foundation projects.

Check [OCG](https://ocgroups.dev/todo) for an existing group. New groups are proposed through GitHub Discussions for Steering Committee review and coordinated with TODO for platform access. Organizers manage the schedule, registration, facilitation, and follow-up. See the [joint community meetup guide](../local-meetups/local-ambassadors-guidelines.md).

## Participation and follow-up

Follow [Linux Foundation policies](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/policies/) and the [event code of conduct](https://events.linuxfoundation.org/about/code-of-conduct/). Keep sessions vendor-neutral and publish language, accessibility, and participation details.

Roundtables follow the [Chatham House Rule](https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/chatham-house-rule): do not record protected discussions or identify speakers in public notes. Record public presentations only with permission and clear notice. Review notes and materials before sharing them under the repository's [CC BY 4.0 license](../LICENSE).

Use the supplied toolkit for speaker preparation, session notes, feedback, and the event recap. Find previous editions in the [OSPOlogy Events archive](records/README.md).
