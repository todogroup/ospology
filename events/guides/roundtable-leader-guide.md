# OSPOlogy roundtable and workshop facilitator guide

Roundtables help participants compare experience and work through shared challenges. Use this guide for both featured discussions and participant-selected unconference topics within the [OSPOlogy framework](../framework.md).

## Prepare

- Agree a clear topic, intended outcome, time limit, and note-taker with organizers.
- For a featured roundtable, prepare questions and a short example in advance.
- For an unconference, help participants propose and select topics before discussion begins.
- Ask about participants' experience and language needs; prepare a structured workshop when background knowledge varies.

## A structured workshop for newcomers

Use a topic from the [OSPO Book](https://ospobook.todogroup.org/) as a starting point. For a 60-minute session:

| Minutes | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| 10 | Introduce the concept and a practical scenario |
| 10 | Invite questions and identify participants' needs |
| 25 | Work in small groups using prepared prompts |
| 10 | Share anonymized lessons and possible actions |
| 5 | Agree next steps and resources |

Offer prompts such as “What would your organization need to get started?” and “Who would need to be involved?” Allow listening and written contributions as well as speaking. The facilitator supplies structure without turning the session into a sales presentation.

## Facilitate

Explain the [Chatham House Rule](https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/chatham-house-rule), code of conduct, and [Linux Foundation antitrust policy](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/legal/antitrust-policy). Do not record protected discussions. Invite different perspectives, prevent one voice from dominating, and keep the conversation within the agreed topic and time.

End with practical lessons, open questions, and agreed follow-up. Confirm which outputs can be shared publicly.

## Review and publish notes

Use the [session notes template](../toolkit/06-Onsite/OSPOlogy-Roundtable-Notes-Template.md). Remove names, affiliations, and details that could identify speakers in protected discussions. Permission to publish a separate case study does not make the rest of a discussion public.

Review the summary before publishing it in the edition's repository directory and linking it from [OSPOlogy Discussions](https://github.com/todogroup/ospology/discussions). Public notes use the repository's [CC BY 4.0 license](../../LICENSE); include only materials participants have permission to share. Credit the public resource without attributing protected statements.
